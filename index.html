NFL player stats, 2009 to 2025: passing peaked, defenses changed

By Rykar Acebo

This report covers 291,750 regular-season player-games from the 2009 through 2025 NFL seasons, taken from the nflverse weekly player stats. It looks at seasons and games rather than careers, so the start of the data never cuts a player off. Passing yards per team-game peaked in 2015 at 259.2 and fell to 225.0 by 2025, while rushing stayed flat. Defenses tell the matching story: interceptions per team-game fell 32% while sacks per pass attempt rose. The dashboard lets you filter the same data, chase advanced stats like EPA per dropback and CPOE, and look up any player.

Finding 01 · Passing peaked, then slid

Passing peaked in 2015 at 259.2 yards per team-game and has slid to 225.0 in 2025, while rushing stayed flat

Adding up every player's passing yards and dividing by team-games, offenses averaged 232.3 passing yards in 2009, climbed to 259.2 in 2015, and fell to 225.0 in 2025, the lowest of the 17 seasons. That is 13.2% below the peak.

Rushing barely moved. Rushing yards per team-game stayed between 108.8 and 121.6 every season, and finished 2025 at 116.9. The slide is a passing story, not a running one.

Finding 02 · Passing peaked, then slid

Fewer quarterbacks reach 4,000 yards: 13 did in 2016, only 6 in 2025

A 4,000-yard passing season used to be common. In 2016, 13 quarterbacks got there, the most of any season. In 2024 it was 6 and in 2025 it was 6.

This counts each player's regular-season total for one season. A season of 17 games (18 weeks from 2021) is enough to make the thresholds comparable, though the extra game from 2021 adds a little room.

Finding 03 · The running back roller coaster

1,000-yard rushers swing widely, from 7 in some seasons to 17 in others

The number of players who ran for 1,000 yards in a season was 17 in 2010, fell to 7 in 2015 and 7 in 2021, then came back to 16 in 2024 and 17 in 2025.

There is no steady trend to explain it. The count is lowest in 2015, 2021 (7) and highest in 2010, 2025 (17).

Finding 04 · The running back roller coaster

The workhorse back nearly disappeared, then returned: 6 backs had 300+ carries in 2009, 0 in 2023, 6 in 2024

A 300-carry season was routine early on: 6 players in 2009 and 7 in 2010. From 2013 to 2022 the count never passed 3, and in 2023 it hit 0.

It came back in 2024 with 6 players and 4 in 2025. The counts are small, so one or two bell-cow backs make a visible difference.

Finding 05 · Receivers pile up catches

100-catch seasons rose even as passing yards fell: 2 in 2010, 12 at the peak

Only 2 players caught 100 passes in 2010. The count reached 12 in 2023 and was 7 in 2025.

These are counts of individual players, so a few high-volume receivers can move the number from one season to the next.

Finding 06 · Defenses: fewer takeaways, more pressure

Interceptions are disappearing: 1.03 per team-game in 2009, 0.70 in 2025

Defenses came up with 1.025 interceptions per team-game in 2009. By 2025 that had fallen to 0.699, a drop of 32%.

The decline is gradual, with no single-season cliff. The data cannot say why it happened.

Finding 07 · Defenses: fewer takeaways, more pressure

Players with 6+ interceptions almost vanished: 11 in 2009, 1 in 2025

In 2009, 11 defenders had six or more interceptions. By 2013 it was 4, and in 2025 only 1 player did it.

The season high in the data is 11 interceptions, by Trevon Diggs in 2021.

Finding 08 · Defenses: fewer takeaways, more pressure

Pass rushes get home more often: sacks per pass attempt rose from 6.4% in 2009 to 7.3% in 2025

Dividing sacks by pass attempts, the sack rate was 6.38% in 2009 and 7.33% in 2025. It peaked at 7.64% in 2023.

Sacks are not counted in pass attempts, so this rate is sacks per attempt, not per dropback. Defenses are taking the ball away less but reaching the quarterback more.

Finding 09 · Defenses: fewer takeaways, more pressure

J.J. Watt owns 4 of the 10 biggest QB-hit seasons, led by 51 in 2014

The top 10 single seasons for quarterback hits include 4 by J.J. Watt, in 2012 (43), 2013 (46), 2014 (51), 2015 (50). The record in the data is 51.

QB hits are recorded on only about a third of defensive player-games and not in a way that lines up with sacks, so treat this as a ranking of recorded hits, not a full count of pressure.

Finding 10 · Defenses: fewer takeaways, more pressure

The sack leader each season ranged from 15.0 (T.J. Watt, 2020) to 23.0 (Myles Garrett, 2025)

Every season has one clear sack leader, with no ties. The lowest leading total was 15.0 by T.J. Watt in 2020. The highest was 23.0 by Myles Garrett in 2025.

T.J. Watt led the league in 3 of the 17 seasons. Hover a bar to see who led that season.

About the data

Where the numbers come from and how they are computed

Source. The nflverse-data stats_player release: weekly player stats, one file per season, licensed CC-BY-4.0. Credit to the nflverse project and its contributors. Definitions of the advanced columns come from the nflverse player stats data dictionary.

What one row is. One row is one player in one game. The cleaned data has 304,515 rows and 69 columns for 17 seasons (2009 to 2025), 8,093 players and 32 teams. The time column is season (and week) and the group columns are player and team. This report and the dashboard's starting view use regular-season rows only, which is 291,750 of them; playoffs can be added with the dashboard's season-type filter.

Rows dropped and other cleaning.

  • Loaded 304,877 player-game rows from 2009 to 2025 (17 seasons)
  • Dropped 362 rows with no player ID, name or position group (about one team-level placeholder row per team per week in the source)
  • Dropped 0 rows with no opponent team
  • Dropped 0 duplicate player-game rows
  • Column def_tackles_for_loss has no non-zero value in 2009: written as NA (not recorded), not 0
  • Column def_tackles_for_loss has no non-zero value in 2010: written as NA (not recorded), not 0
  • Column def_tackles_for_loss has no non-zero value in 2011: written as NA (not recorded), not 0
  • Kept 4,536 rows whose kept stat columns are all zero (player appears in the source game record without a tracked stat here). They count as appearances.

Tackles for loss are not recorded in 2009 to 2011 and are left blank (not zero) there. Player names are not unique, so players are identified by player_id. Full details are in data/DATA_QUALITY.md and data/DATA_DICTIONARY.md.

How every number is computed.

  • Team-game: one team in one game. Per-team-game rates add up every player's stat for the season and divide by the number of regular-season team-games (32 teams, 16 games each through 2020, 17 from 2021, with one fewer game in 2022).
  • Player-season counts (4,000-yard passers, 1,000-yard rushers, 300-carry backs, 100-catch receivers, players with 6+ interceptions): each player's regular-season total for one season is compared to the threshold, and players who met it are counted once. A player who changed teams has one total across teams.
  • Interceptions per team-game is defensive interceptions divided by team-games.
  • Sacks per pass attempt is defensive sacks (halves count) divided by pass attempts. Sacks are not part of attempts.
  • QB hits and sack leaders are single-season player totals. Ranking ties are broken by player name. No two players tied for the sack lead in any season.
  • Advanced stats on the dashboard: EPA per dropback is passing EPA divided by attempts plus sacks. ANY/A is (passing yards + 20 x touchdowns - 45 x interceptions + sack yards lost) divided by (attempts + sacks), with sack yards stored as negatives. CPOE is each game's CPOE weighted by that game's attempts. RACR is receiving yards divided by receiving air yards. Target share, air-yards share and WOPR are averages over games in which the player was targeted. Each dashboard measure states its definition under the chart.

Limits. Stats are only as good as the source; tackles depend on stat-crew scoring and are not used here. Metrics that need play-by-play, Next Gen Stats or Pro Football Reference data (EPA allowed, pressure rate, time to throw, missed tackles) are not in this dataset. Reasons for the trends, such as rule changes, are outside the data and are not tested here.

Every number on this page is computed by scripts/build_report.py from data/seasons/, and scripts/check_numbers.js recomputes them with the dashboard's own code.
